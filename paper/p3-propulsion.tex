\subsubsection{Effective Specific Impulse}
We compare effective specific impulses of 300, 330, and 360 s, with maximum thrust, minimum throttle, gimbal limits, aerodynamic coefficients, and oxygen-to-methane mass ratio unchanged. Each changed case reoptimizes inventory, phase durations, and controls within the P2 single-ignition family. The 330 s row uses the selected nominal result. All three candidates satisfy the adopted touchdown conditions in full-flight replay at steps of 0.005 s and 0.0025 s; Table~\ref{tab:p3-isp-budget} uses the finer replay. The candidates are local solutions, not proven global minima.

\midrule
300 & 28.781 & 23.701 & 5.079 & 6.396 & 22.385 \\
330 & 26.228 & 21.150 & 5.078 & 5.828 & 20.400 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Relative to 330 s, accepted inventory rises by 2.552 t (9.73\%) at 300 s and falls by 2.029 t (7.74\%) at 360 s. Higher specific impulse reduces consumption at a prescribed thrust, but total inventory also includes the retained amount, and the changed mass affects the descent. Replanning the complete trajectory therefore differs from simply scaling inventory inversely with specific impulse. The range 24.199--28.781 t describes these known-efficiency designs, not a confidence interval. Holding thrust and mixture ratio fixed is an effective-parameter approximation rather than a thermochemically coupled engine redesign.
