\subsection{Spherical-Earth Correction}
\label{sec:sphere}
For the reference calculation we replace the flat geometry with a nonrotating spherical Earth of radius $R_E$. Write $r=R_E+h$ and $s=R_E\phi$, where $s$ is surface arc distance. Velocities $v_x$ and $v_h$ are the physical components along the local horizontal and radial directions, which turn as the booster moves downrange. The state keeps its seven components, with $x$ replaced by $s$. Let $a_x=F_x/m$ and $a_h=F_h/m$ denote the non-gravitational accelerations. Differentiating the moving basis gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:sphere}
 \dot s&=\frac{R_E}{r}v_x,&\dot h&=v_h,\\
 \dot v_x&=a_x-\frac{v_xv_h}{r},&
 \dot v_h&=a_h-g(h)+\frac{v_x^2}{r}.
\end{align}
The last terms in the velocity equations are the curvature accelerations, and the factor $R_E/r$ converts horizontal speed at altitude into distance along the surface.

We keep $\omega=\dot\theta$ as the pitch rate relative to the local horizontal. Because the local horizontal rotates at $-v_x/r$ in the flight plane, the inertial body rate is $\omega_I=\omega-v_x/r$, and the strip velocity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:strip} becomes $u_n+\omega_I \xi_i$. The angular equation is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spherepitch}
 \dot\omega=\frac{M_a+M_T}{I_d}+\frac{a_x}{r}-\frac{2v_xv_h}{r^2},
\end{equation}
where the last two terms come from differentiating $v_x/r$. With them, a torque-free body keeps a fixed inertial attitude while its pitch measured from the local horizontal changes as it travels downrange.

For contact with the sea-level deck, the longitudinal section of the cylinder is a rectangle in the flight plane. Its point nearest the Earth's center has body coordinates
\begin{equation}
 a_* = \operatorname{clip}(-r\sin\theta,-L/2,L/2),\qquad
 b_* = \operatorname{clip}(-r\cos\theta,-R_b,R_b),
\end{equation}
and first contact occurs when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spherecontact}
 f_s=\sqrt{(a_*\cos\theta-b_*\sin\theta)^2+(r+a_*\sin\theta+b_*\cos\theta)^2}-R_E-h_{deck}=0.
\end{equation}
The ship is a fixed point on the surface, given by its arc coordinate. Within the 5 m position tolerance the local vertical turns by less than $10^{-6}$ rad, so the deck at the contact point is level in the local frame.
